You are a Product Manager on the Growth Team at your chosen product.

One of the problems the team is tackling is the following:

As AI tools become increasingly capable, users are relying on it for more complex and high-stakes tasks such as writing, coding, analysis, research, and career preparation.

However, a major challenge is emerging:

AI-generated outputs often look convincing, polished, and complete—even when they contain weak reasoning, missing context, or subtle inaccuracies.

Most users struggle to determine:

Whether an output is actually good

Whether it can be trusted

What might be missing or uncertain

When they should rely on their own judgment instead of the AI’s output

As a result:

Users either over-trust AI outputs or become overly skeptical of them

Weak outputs propagate into real work

Users fail to develop strong evaluation skills

Trust in AI systems becomes unstable and inconsistent

Your solution must focus on:

Helping users evaluate and develop confidence in AI-generated outputs without replacing human judgment.

The solution should explicitly address:

1. Helping Users Understand Output Quality

Help users evaluate:

Correctness

Completeness

Reasoning quality

Usefulness

Uncertainty

Your solution should acknowledge that “Good output” is contextual and subjective.

2. Supporting Human Judgment (Not Replacing It)

AI tools should:

Assist users in evaluating outputs

Surface uncertainty or alternative perspectives

Encourage critical thinking

AI tools should NOT:

Present itself as the ultimate authority

Encourage blind trust

Remove ambiguity where ambiguity naturally exists

3. Confidence Calibration

Help users:

Avoid overconfidence in polished outputs

Avoid excessive skepticism that reduces productivity

Build better intuition around AI-generated work over time

4. Making AI Reasoning More Legible

Help users better understand:

Why an output was generated

What assumptions were made

What information may be missing

The solution may involve AI evaluating AI outputs however the system you design must avoid becoming another opaque “black box”
